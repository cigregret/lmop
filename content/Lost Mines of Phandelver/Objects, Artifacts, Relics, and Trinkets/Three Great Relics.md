Three Great Relics, said to have the power to open the heavens and raze the world anew. The Three Great Relics names are never to be spoken aloud, for mere mention of their true names has the power to create minor catastrophe, or announce yourself to those who hide in the shade. These artifacts of unimaginable power are rumored to hail from the [[Feywilde]], yet their purpose is to open a gateway to [[Mount Celestia]].

The Three Great Relics' full names are unknown at this time, but they are referred to only as follows:
- The Book
- The Blade
- The Glass
Should a being of any status refer to them as even part of their true names (the Tome, the Sword, and the Mirror, respectively), it is said to be akin to requesting the divine intervention of catastrophe.

There are said to be [[Heretic Cultists of Bahamut]] who seek to find these millennia-old relics to destroy the world by performing a ritual somewhere in the [[Moonshae Isles]].

There is said to be an indecipherable map imbued with Fey magic (known as the [[Map of Indirection]]) that leads its holder to the Book, held currently by [[MGs]] of the [[Lord's Alliance]] and [[Phandalin Fellows]]. The other two Great Relics' locations or hints are unknown, but one is said to reside within [[Wave Echo Cave]].


#### The Tome of Temporal Paintings (The Book)
The Book is a source of infinite knowledge, said to drive anyone who attempts to read it mad. As the legends state, whether the reader be god or mortal they will spend the last few seconds of their life with the knowledge of everything that has happened, is happening, and is guaranteed to come. However, it is also said that the Book reflects it's current holder's journey, and will occasionally spit out a page of the future to come. These pages, while undocumented outside of this myth, are said to be beautifully illustrated paintings that depict a particular instance near in the future of the holder's current timeline.

[[MGs]] has a map from the [[Feywilde]] that somehow shows the location of the Book, yet is indecipherable to all who look at it. Looking at the map for more than five seconds will magically put the creature investigating it to sleep. 

#### The Mirror of Hazy Whispers (The Glass)
The Glass is a pocket folding mirror, with intricate engravings upon its wooden shell. If one is to open the mirror and peer inside, they will see nothing but wisps of smoke that curl around the non-reflective glass. If a creature stares for too long, they begin to lose memories and have the memories of other onlookers implanted into their head. Should they look for even longer, their entire soul will be siphoned through this memory transfer, and the lost soul of another observer will inhabit their body. It is said that many have attempted to live forever through using this mirror in a cultish ritual, where they intentionally siphon their soul to be artificially reincarnated into a later individual.

Stands in Smoke (aka [[Smoke]]), the tiger tabaxi that rules the [[Waterdeep]] underworld, currently has this artifact. He knows its power, and seeks to guard it from the world to never be found again. However, he has lived for hundreds of years thanks to this artifact and will continue to do so, seeing it as the ultimate duty to protect Fae'run through these means.

#### The Sword of Blind Fog (The Blade)
The Blade is only the broken hilt of a sword, yet there are no records of a full blade ever having existed.  It was forged by a druidic smithy ([[Fry's]] master), commissioned by [[The Prince of Fools]], an Archfey. If one attempts to wield the sword, they become slowly corroded with necrotic energy; continuing to wield it results in the user becoming a conscious undead who cannot die. The only way to put someone who has entered this zombie-like state to rest is to magically cut the tether between the Blade and the soul of its user. The Blade, however, will refuse to let go under any circumstances, and the level of complex magic necessary is akin to surgical precision. 

As legend states, only one person has ever wielded the Blade: a demigod of their people. They swore to forever protect the memory of their loved ones, and willingly accepted the curse to endlessly fend off any intruders to this enclosed holy ground. When the sun shines brightest, its solar purity reaches the Tombguard and harshly awakens it from perpetual slumber, burning what is left of its corroded flesh. The demigod roars, drowning out the waves of no source, and hides in an arena outside of time and space. This is the power of the Blade.
